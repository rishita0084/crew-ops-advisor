02 The problem 05:00Z

A captain calls in sick at five in the morning.

The controller has minutes to work out who is affected, which flights are now at risk, who can legally be moved, who can be reached, and what it costs — across rosters, duty clocks, reserve registers and a regulatory rulebook on separate screens.

Detecting is easy

The broken flight is obvious. The four that break next are not.

Legality is arithmetic

Sixty duty hours in seven calendar days. An approximate answer is a violation.

Expertise doesn't scale

It lives in one senior controller's head, and degrades exactly when it matters.

03 Why the obvious build fails Design

Put the roster in the prompt and the model will answer confidently.

It will also, sometimes, be wrong — and a fluent wrong duty-hour calculation is worse than no answer, because a controller has no way to see it happen.

Can Captain C-2087 cover P-2291 from 15 Sep?

RULE-DUTY-02 · FAIL

accrued 51.83h  +  proposed 9.50h  =  61.33h  vs  limit 60.00h

Over by 1h20m on 2026-09-15. That figure is arithmetic over a 28-day history, a planned roster and a calendar-day window. It is not a thing to approximate.

Every number on this slide came out of the engine, not out of a model.

10 Insight · simultaneous vacancies One body, two aircraft

Two sick calls are not two problems.

Solve them separately and each picks the cheapest legal reserve — the same reserve. The plan looks optimal and cannot be flown.

Solved separately

P-2205 → C-3305  ₹18,500
P-2212 → C-3305  ₹18,500

Total ₹37,000 — and physically impossible.

Solved jointly

P-2205 → C-3305  ₹18,500
P-2212 → C-1017  ₹24,000

Total ₹42,500 — matches the answer key.

Cheapest combination subject to every vacancy getting a distinct crew member. Exhaustive over a cost-sorted shortlist, so it is provably optimal — and every step stays explainable, which a solver would not be.

11 Explainability Deliverable 3

The system structurally cannot state a fact it did not compute.

Every engine operation appends to an evidence ledger — source, fact, value. After the model writes its sentence, a verifier extracts every crew id, flight number, pairing, rule id and money figure from the prose and checks each one against that ledger. Unbacked claims are flagged in the UI, not silently dropped.

A real escape it caught

The model wrote C‑3310 using a Unicode non-breaking hyphen. The C-\d{4} check didn't match, so it passed silently. Ids are now dash-normalised before checking — with a test.

And when it doesn't know

It returns cannot_answer with what was missing. The brief scores that above a confident guess. So do we.

This is not “we prompt it carefully”. It is a gate the prose has to pass.

One explicit checker per question — the exact field, the exact value. No substring matching. Answer keys are fixtures only: nothing in the application reads them and nothing caches their answers.

Our first grader was lazy, and it hid four real bugs

It accepted ~80% of expected values appearing anywhere in the output, and reported a clean sheet. Making the checks exact surfaced, immediately: two aircraft assigned the same captain · closures modelled as crew shortages · exclusion reasons missing their rule ids · a recovery intent that never matched. A weak test is worse than no test, because it turns “unknown” into “verified”.

13 Honest failure Deliverable 6

Scenario S4 — where we disagree with your answer key.

The key says

₹75,000

Fresh complement re-crews DX404 after the delay.

We say

₹114,000

The same callout, plus 6 × ₹6,500 positioning.

DX404 departs MAA. Every crew member in the dataset is based at BLR or DEL — there are zero MAA-based crew. A fresh complement has to be flown there (feasible on DX453, arriving 09:00Z against an 11:45Z report), and costs.json prices that at ₹6,500 a head.

We chose not to quietly match the key. The engine itemises both parts — the callout subtotal matches you exactly — and the scorecard tags S4 as a divergence whether or not it passes. We may be wrong. But silently reporting a number we believe is ₹39,000 short is the exact failure this system exists to prevent.

Also honest: swap-chain search only engages once direct cover drops to two or fewer, which on this generously crewed week means telling it which crew are already committed. It is bounded at beam 8, depth 3, so a recovery needing four coordinated moves would be missed.

14 Two doors, one engine System of action

A crew desk does not want another tab.

The console answers over HTTP. Any MCP client answers over stdio. Both land on the same dispatch — one rules engine, one cost model, one source of truth — and a test asserts the two return identical answers, so they cannot drift apart.

Our console

Ask, read the answer, expand the reasoning. A watch list runs unprompted and found the dataset's one planted compliance breach — C-5417 rostered past a training expiry — without being asked.

Claude Desktop, an IDE, another agent

16 tools, evidence attached to every result. Airlines already fund MCP-exposed crew tooling for quals and pay. Nobody has put the recovery reasoning behind that interface. That is the layer this adds.

Nothing listens on a port for MCP — the client owns the process. It keeps working when the API is stopped.
